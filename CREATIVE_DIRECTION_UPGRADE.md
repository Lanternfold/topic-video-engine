# Creative Direction Upgrade: Psychology, UX, Kinetic Design & Personal Signature

## Role

Act as a senior motion-design director, visual communication designer, UX specialist, short-form video strategist, and creative technologist.

Your task is to upgrade the creative-direction system of my existing vertical-video renderer. The goal is to produce distinctive, attention-grabbing, highly legible short-form videos for Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.

Do not optimise merely for videos that look polished. Optimise for videos that interrupt scrolling, communicate quickly, sustain curiosity, make information easy to understand, and leave the viewer with a memorable idea.

The creative should feel professionally art-directed rather than like a generic AI-generated video template.

## 1. Core creative philosophy

Prioritise the following, in order:

1. Audience attention and curiosity.
2. Story clarity and factual credibility.
3. Visual hierarchy and readability on mobile screens.
4. Pacing, progression and satisfying reveals.
5. Distinctive art direction appropriate to the specific story.
6. A subtle creator personality.
7. Production efficiency.

The video itself must remain the star. Personal style should contribute a small, recognisable fingerprint without turning the channel into a personal vlog or imposing a rigid identity on every video.

Do not assume that dark, cinematic, neon, minimalist, or highly colourful design is universally best. Select the treatment that serves the particular story and its intended audience.

## 2. Apply psychology and UX principles deliberately

Use established design principles rather than simplistic colour-psychology stereotypes.

### Attention and visual hierarchy

- Establish one dominant visual focal point at any given moment.
- Use contrast, size, placement, isolation and motion to direct attention.
- Give the opening frame an immediately understandable visual idea.
- Use visual novelty when it reinforces the story, not as random decoration.
- Reveal information progressively when doing so creates meaningful curiosity.
- Use visual changes to signal changes in the argument, evidence, or narrative.
- Avoid multiple competing focal points, unnecessary animation and decorative clutter.
- Design every scene to remain understandable when viewed quickly on a phone.

### Colour selection

Choose a palette based on the subject, emotional register, audience expectations, readability and desired attention hierarchy.

Consider:
- Hue: useful for differentiation and contextual associations.
- Saturation: use vivid colours for energy and emphasis where appropriate.
- Lightness and contrast: ensure important information remains legible.
- Colour relationships: create deliberate harmony or tension.
- Semantic consistency: use colours consistently for meaningful categories within a video.
- Accessibility: do not rely on colour alone to communicate meaning.

Treat colour associations as contextual tendencies, not universal psychological laws. Never claim that a colour will independently make a video viral or increase retention.

### Starting palette for high-energy editorial content

Use this palette as an initial hypothesis, not a mandatory template:

- Ink: #171820
- Paper: #F6F0E6
- Coral: #FF5C35
- Signal yellow: #FFD447
- Mint: #65D6C4

Use ink or paper as the dominant foundation. Choose one primary accent based on the story. Add a second accent only when it provides a clear functional or visual benefit.

For example:
- Coral can emphasise a defect, contradiction, warning or reveal.
- Yellow can highlight a key figure, question or moment of discovery.
- Mint can distinguish verified information, successful validation or a contrasting data category, provided its meaning is clearly established.

These assignments are conventions for this design system, not inherent meanings of the colours.

Allow bright, light-background, dark, muted, documentary, or experimental treatments when the subject calls for them. Do not force every video into the starting palette.

### Typography and mobile readability

- Use typography as a storytelling instrument, not merely as a subtitle layer.
- Establish a clear hierarchy between hook, supporting copy, evidence, annotations and source attribution.
- Prefer strong, legible typefaces and deliberate weight contrast.
- Use kinetic typography to emphasise important words or create a reveal.
- Keep essential text brief enough to read within its on-screen duration.
- Avoid long paragraphs, tiny captions, excessive uppercase text and unnecessary font changes.
- Keep essential information within mobile-safe areas, clear of platform interface overlays.
- Maintain strong text-background contrast.
- Never sacrifice readability for stylistic novelty.

### Motion and pacing

- Use movement to communicate emphasis, sequence, change or discovery.
- Combine fast moments with brief pauses when a pause increases comprehension or anticipation.
- Make transitions reflect relationships between scenes or ideas.
- Use scale, cropping, reveals, wipes, directional movement and typography changes intentionally.
- Avoid applying the same transition to every scene.
- Avoid continuous motion that leaves the viewer no time to process information.
- Ensure that a viewer can follow the story even without sound.

### Sound and audio

- Treat narration, sound effects, music and silence as separate creative decisions.
- Use sound to reinforce transitions, discoveries and emotional shifts.
- Avoid loud effects on every cut or music that overwhelms narration.
- Recommend specific audio only when its identity and availability have been verified.
- Distinguish a creative music recommendation from verified current platform-trend evidence.
- Respect platform-specific music licensing and availability. The renderer should not embed unlicensed commercial music.

## 3. Add a subtle personal signature

The creator's personality should contribute approximately 5% of the creative experience, as a guiding principle rather than a literal measurable ratio.

The intended sensibility is:

- Curious and observant.
- Interested in how things work beneath the surface.
- Comfortable with precise explanations and unexpected connections.
- Occasionally dry, understated or quietly funny.
- Thoughtful without becoming sentimental or preachy.
- Visually considered without making every video look like an art film.

Potential expressions include:
- One understated observation that reframes the subject.
- A dry aside at an appropriate moment.
- A surprising connection between seemingly unrelated ideas.
- A deliberate pause before an important reveal.
- A particularly elegant diagram, transition or visual metaphor.
- An ending that leaves the viewer with a useful or intriguing thought.

These are optional creative devices, not mandatory ingredients.

Do not force poetry, philosophical endings, Hinglish, personal anecdotes, catchphrases, jazz references, or recurring visual motifs into every video.

Do not imitate a particular creator. Build an original style from the story and the creator's broad sensibility.

If the story benefits from a straightforward, energetic or humorous treatment, prioritise that over the personal signature.

## 4. Select the visual treatment per video

For every new video, analyse the subject and recommend a specific art direction before rendering.

Consider treatments such as:

- Kinetic editorial: energetic typography, bold contrast, expressive transitions.
- Investigative: evidence-focused compositions, annotations, highlighted details and progressive reveals.
- Data-driven: animated charts, maps, diagrams and carefully staged comparisons.
- Documentary: atmospheric imagery, measured pacing and contextual details.
- Playful explanatory: surprising visual metaphors, clean diagrams and understated humour.
- Experimental: unconventional composition or motion when it genuinely improves the story.

These are a library of possible approaches, not fixed channel categories.

The final direction may combine multiple approaches. Choose based on the story, not the desire to use every technique.

When reliable current platform evidence is available, use it to inform the decision. If trend data is unavailable, stale or unverified, explicitly state that limitation. Never invent trending audio, platform performance statistics or audience preferences.

## 5. Design for a compelling short-form narrative

For a 30–60 second video, consider this structure:

1. Hook: establish a surprising question, contradiction or visual.
2. Context: give the minimum information needed to understand the problem.
3. Development: reveal evidence, complications or an unexpected pattern.
4. Payoff: show the discovery or explain what it means.
5. Exit: finish with a memorable insight or a natural conclusion.

Adapt the structure when another approach better serves the story.

Do not manufacture suspense by withholding essential information unnecessarily. Do not exaggerate a claim simply to strengthen the hook. Every visual reveal and narrative beat must be supported by the actual story.

## 6. Integrate with the existing renderer

Inspect the existing project before changing anything.

Preserve:
- The existing renderer and its working scene implementations.
- The established 1080 × 1920 vertical format and 30 fps default.
- Existing profiles and creative-brief schemas where possible.
- The current human-approval gate.
- The separation between trend research, creative decision-making and rendering.
- Existing dependencies unless a change has a clear, justified benefit.

Implement the smallest useful set of changes that enables the creative-direction system to express the principles above.

Prefer configuration-driven creative controls and reusable scene behaviours over hardcoded styling. Do not build an elaborate agent framework, introduce unnecessary dependencies, or rewrite working code without a demonstrated need.

The renderer must consume an approved creative brief. It must not silently override the approved direction or publish content.

## 7. Required creative brief fields

Where compatible with the current schema, each video brief should specify:

- Story premise and target audience.
- Primary audience curiosity or question.
- Desired emotional progression.
- Opening hook and its visual treatment.
- Chosen art direction and rationale.
- Palette with semantic colour assignments.
- Typography hierarchy and animation behaviour.
- Scene-by-scene visual focal points.
- Motion intensity and pacing.
- Narration, sound-effect and music requirements.
- Mobile readability and safe-area constraints.
- Evidence sources, factual claims and verification status.
- Optional personal-signature moment and why it belongs.
- Accessibility considerations.
- Human approval status.

Do not invent fields blindly. Inspect the current schema and extend it compatibly.

## 8. Evaluate creative quality

Create a practical review checklist that evaluates:

- Immediate comprehension of the opening.
- Strength and clarity of the visual hierarchy.
- Mobile text readability.
- Effective use of contrast and colour.
- Whether movement reinforces meaning.
- Narrative progression and payoff.
- Factual accuracy and evidence traceability.
- Originality relative to generic AI video templates.
- Whether the personal signature feels natural rather than forced.
- Technical correctness of the rendered output.

Use the checklist for human review, not as a claim that an automated score predicts virality.

Where possible, generate preview frames or contact sheets so creative treatments can be reviewed quickly.

## 9. Execution instructions

First inspect the existing project and identify the smallest necessary changes.

Then:
1. Propose the changes and explain why they are necessary.
2. Implement the changes without disrupting working functionality.
3. Update or add relevant tests.
4. Validate schema compatibility and human-approval enforcement.
5. Render a short sample if the existing tools permit it.
6. Report exactly what changed, what was tested and what remains unverified.

Do not build a complete new video or redesign the entire renderer as part of this task.

Success means the system can create more compelling, legible, story-specific videos with a subtle personal fingerprint, while remaining inexpensive, configurable and human-approved.
